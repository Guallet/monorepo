# Guallet Design Context

## Design Context

### Users
Mildly tech-savvy individuals who want meaningful control over their personal finances. They use a budgeting app regularly — checking balances, reviewing transactions, tracking spending patterns. They're comfortable with technology but not necessarily developers. They open Guallet on a weekday morning with coffee, or in the evening after paying bills. The product is self-hosted, so there's an implied trust relationship: this person chose to run their own instance.

### Brand Personality
**Three words:** methodical, unhurried, trustworthy

The interface should feel like a quiet expert — confident without being loud, structured without being cold. No urgency. No marketing pressure. Just clear, reliable information delivered with precision. Think: the inner pages of a well-designed annual report, or a thoughtfully designed banking app that respects your intelligence.

**Emotional goals:** Calm control + sharp clarity. Users should feel *in command* when they look at their data — not overwhelmed, not anxious. Numbers should be legible and contextual. The UI chrome should recede so the financial content is the hero.

### Aesthetic Direction
**Theme:** Light mode. Used across varied times of day, but the primary context is daytime or well-lit environments. Warm off-white surfaces, not clinical white.

**Visual reference:** Coinbase — clean confidence, restrained palette, strong typographic hierarchy, generous negative space, financial-grade seriousness without stuffiness. Data is presented directly without decoration.

**Anti-patterns to avoid:**
- Anxious/busy layouts with too many competing elements
- Electric cyan or neon accents (even though the current brand uses cyan)
- The "dashboard template" look: identical card grids, hero metrics with gradient accents
- Anything that looks like a generic SaaS admin panel
- Rounded corners with drop shadows on everything

**Palette direction:**
- Base: warm off-white surfaces (tinted slightly toward the brand blue, not pure white)
- Neutrals: stone-warm greys with a blue tint — not cold blue-grey, not warm yellow-grey
- Primary accent: the deep brand blue (#005EB8) used sparingly — for active states, CTAs, and key data points only
- Secondary text/borders: muted, receding — not grey-on-grey, but clearly subordinate
- Numbers showing negative amounts: muted terracotta/rust, not alarming red
- Numbers showing positive: muted sage, not celebration green

**Typography direction:**
- Headings/display: **Epilogue** (editorial, structured, confident at large sizes — not a default choice)
- UI text and body: **Figtree** (warm geometric, excellent number rendering, slightly humanist — not on the reflex list)
- Both available on Google Fonts
- Tabular figures (`font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums`) required everywhere financial data appears

### Design Principles

1. **Data is the hero.** The UI exists to frame financial information, not compete with it. Reduce visual noise; let numbers breathe.

2. **Calm before clarity.** If a layout feels busy or tense, simplify before you clarify. Unhurried pacing — generous spacing, measured rhythm — comes before dense information hierarchy.

3. **Trustworthy restraint.** One accent color, used rarely. No decorative gradients. No sparklines that exist just to look sophisticated. Every visual element must earn its place.

4. **Deliberate hierarchy.** Typography and spacing do the heavy lifting — not color, not shadows, not borders. Size, weight, and whitespace create structure.

5. **Adaptive without amputating.** On smaller screens, adapt the layout — don't hide financial data or reduce it to a skeleton. The user came here to see their finances.
